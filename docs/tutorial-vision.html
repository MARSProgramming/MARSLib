Vision Fusion & Filtration

In the FRC 2026: REBUILT game, absolute field localization is critical for path planning and automatic scoring. MARSLib's `MARSVision` subsystem combines data from multiple cameras (Limelight, PhotonVision) and strictly filters out "hallucinations" using techniques pioneered by elite teams like 254 (The Cheesy Poofs).

1. The Strict Rejection Filters

Vision poses are frequently completely wrong during high-speed, dynamic gameplay. Before a vision measurement is ever sent to SwerveDrive's pose estimator, it must survive five strict boundary checks:

  • Z-Height Hallucinations: If the pose estimates the robot is flying (Z > 0.5m) or under the floor, it's rejected.
  • Out of Bounds: If the pose is tracked outside the physical 16.5m x 8m field (+ 0.5m margin), it's rejected.
  • Motion Blur: Cameras suffer rolling shutter during fast spins. If the gyro reports > 120°/s yaw rate, vision is entirely blocked until the robot slows down.
  • Chassis Beaching (Pitch/Roll): PnP geometry assumes your camera is level relative to the floor. If you ride over an obstacle and tilt > 15°, the pose is rejected.
  • Ambiguity: Low quality single-tag solutions are discarded (Limelight MegaTag2 suppresses this natively).

2. Quadratic Standard Deviation Scaling

Vision poses should mathematically never be trusted equally. A pose taken from 1 meter away is crisp and geometrically sound, but a pose from 6 meters away is tiny on the camera sensor—1 pixel of error dramatically shifts the calculated location.

MARSLib enforces Quadratic StdDev Scaling. We don't scale linearly (distance * 0.1); we scale quadratically (`Math.pow(distance, 2) * 0.05`). This means trust in vision decays exponentially at long range, stopping the robot from making violent odometry correction jumps when it relies on a far-away, pixelated AprilTag.

MegaTag / Multi-Tag Boost

When multiple AprilTags are visible in the same frame, geometric ambiguity essentially drops to zero because the PnP solver can lock onto two separate coordinate reference planes simultaneously. When tagCount > 1, our code automatically scales down the standard deviations by x0.1, dramatically tightening the Pose Estimator's trust regardless of distance.

3. Simulating Imperfection

Running code natively in simulation is dangerous if everything is 'too perfect'. Out of the box, PhotonLib's `VisionSystemSim` feeds perfect poses to the robot. To ensure tuning works globally, the `AprilTagVisionIOSim` in MARSLib purposefully injects:

  • Gaussian Noise: StdDev matched jitter scaled by distance directly into the simulated 3D coordinates.
  • Dropped Frames: A 5% chance every frame that no pose is returned, simulating CAN/Network congestion.
  • Latency: Simulates an artificial 10ms-30ms phase delay offset representing true RaspberryPi latency.
