At **MARS 2614**, we recognize that genuine growth and breakthrough innovation do not happen when things are easy. That is why **Leaning Into Discomfort** is a foundational pillar of our team's ethos and the MARSLib framework.

## Embracing the Unknown
FRC software engineering is inherently difficult. You will face concepts that feel insurmountable:
- Transitioning from simple `TimedRobot` architectures to advanced **State Machines**.
- Trusting **Zero-Mocking** simulations when you want to just deploy code to the robot immediately.
- Adopting **Zero-Allocation** logic rather than continually relying on the garbage collector.

It is uncomfortable to learn that the "easy way" will ultimately bottleneck the robot in competition. Leaning into discomfort means choosing the mathematically correct, robust path—even when it requires hours of reading documentation and tuning SysId feedforwards.

## The Growth Mindset
Discomfort is the physical sensation of your brain creating new neural pathways. We encourage students to:
1. **Take on Hard Tickets:** Volunteer for the subsystem you don't instantly understand.
2. **Fail Forward:** When a simulation test crashes or the robot behaves erratically, do not retreat to old code. Analyze the AdvantageKit log, diagnose the root cause, and learn from it.
3. **Ask for Help:** Admitting you do not understand a concept is uncomfortable, but it is the prerequisite for **Discovery** and **Teamwork**.

By leaning into discomfort, we ensure that our students emerge from MARS not just as capable developers, but as resilient engineers ready to tackle the hardest problems the world has to offer.
